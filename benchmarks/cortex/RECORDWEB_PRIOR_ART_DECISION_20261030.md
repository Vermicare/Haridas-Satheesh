## Primary evidence

- The [W3C RecordWeb Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/recordweb/) was publicly launched on **2026-07-16** ([W3C launch announcement](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-council/2026Jul/0000.html)). Its stated mission is verifiable institutional records governance, exploring composition of decentralized identifiers, verifiable credentials and PROV-O. The group describes **proposed future** explainer and protocol deliverables; this is a community-led initiative, **not** a published W3C Recommendation or evidence of implementation/interoperability.
- The established [W3C PROV-O Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) (2013-04-30) describes provenance entities, activities, agents and derivation. PROV-O relationships alone do **not** prove historical truth, approval validity, signer identity, or legal attribution.

These are discovery references only; **no contact was made** and no relationship is implied.

## Decision-changing implication for CORTEX

CORTEX's author-created **fictional** PROV projection is not an original general-purpose provenance standard. The institutional-records problem and potential DID/credential/PROV composition are actively being explored in an open W3C-hosted community. Before proposing a new vocabulary, product positioning, or external review, prepare a **side-by-side semantic gap matrix**: (1) document derivation; (2) decision supersession versus document revision; (3) identity and authority proof; (4) time/freeze/retention; (5) revocation; (6) source-span and correction lineage. Mark each as `existing standard`, `domain-specific mapping`, or `unverified`. Use the group's future drafts only if actually published and version-pinned.

**What this does not establish:** CORTEX novelty, patentability, compliance, standard endorsement, deployed provenance, semantic correctness, independent annotations, human correction burden, or adoption.
